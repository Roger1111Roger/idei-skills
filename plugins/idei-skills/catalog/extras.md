# Do not forget

Short reminders attached to steps. These are project rules, not ready-made methods from sources; the source pages confirm individual principles only.

## e1 · Goal and decision

What decision will be made based on the research results and who makes it. Without this it is unclear what counts as a result.

- Steps: 1. Hypothesis
- Source of principles: https://www.producttalk.org/experiment-design/

## e2 · Idea prioritization

ICE (impact, confidence, ease) or RICE (reach, impact, confidence, effort). It helps choose which ideas to check first. Weak spot: confidence is easily overstated without evidence, and the score does not replace a decision.

- Steps: 2. Assumptions and risks
- Source of principles: https://productplan.com/glossary/rice-scoring-model https://www.koji.so/docs/ice-prioritization-framework

## e5 · Resources and deadlines

Budget, time for the experiment and stop conditions. They are set together with the success criteria. One company's practice: a time limit, a cost ceiling and a stop condition before starting; no basic evidence by the deadline means changing course or closing.

- Steps: 3. Success criteria
- Source of principles: https://leaddev.com/?p=33386

## e3 · Competitors and alternatives

What people do now instead of your solution. Including the option of “doing nothing”.

- Steps: 4. Desk research
- Source of principles: https://www.aprildunford.com/post/positioning-and-competition

## e10 · AI in research

Skills, search and subagents speed up the collection of sources and save tokens on routine work.

- Steps: 4. Desk research
- Source of principles: none, not checked against a primary source

## e8 · Hypothesis log

Record every hypothesis and result, especially a negative one: even “mistakes” often turn out to be important. Do not erase errors but cross them out legibly, and date every entry.

- Steps: 8. Decision
- Source of principles: https://web.physics.indiana.edu/courses/p451/s15/index_files/Page480.htm

## e9 · Stakeholders

Who checks the intermediate conclusions, and when. Boehm's and McConnell's data on requirements defects in software: fixing late costs more than fixing early, so users are involved as early as possible.

- Steps: 8. Decision
- Source of principles: https://stevemcconnell.com/articles/an-ounce-of-prevention/

## e6 · Data and bias

Sources, reliability, biases: sampling, confirmation, measurement, observer, survivorship. Each has a remedy: random sampling, blind checking, calibration, accounting for failures.

- Steps: 7. Measurement
- Source of principles: https://www.statology.org/5-types-of-data-bias-and-how-to-address-them/

## e7 · Reproducibility

Reproducibility: the same data and code give the same result. Replicability: a new study with new data gives a consistent result (definitions of the US National Academies). This requires clear methods and open data and code.

- Steps: 6. Mini-experiment
- Source of principles: https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2019/09/reproducibility-and-replicability-in-research

## e4 · Law and ethics

Licenses, personal data, copyright, platform restrictions. Checked before an experiment on people and data. Under the DfE rules for user research: informed consent, anonymization in analysis, restricted access to data. Licenses and copyright are not confirmed by this page.

- Steps: 5. Assessment: three axes
- Source of principles: https://user-research.education.gov.uk/standards-and-principles/personal-data-handling

## e11 · Honesty of evidence

Project rule: each statement is labeled by its origin (a fact with a source, an observation, an assumption or an estimate). Sources that did not open are recorded, not skipped. Each conclusion has a check that can fail. Basis: honesty, rigor and transparency in good research practice.

- Steps: 4. Desk research; 7. Measurement
- Source of principles: https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/research/research-resources/research-integrity-good-research-practice/introduction-research-integrity-good-research-practice.html

## e12 · Different viewpoints

Examine the idea through the eyes of several roles: future user, developer, tester, skeptic, lawyer, financier. Each role asks its own questions, the answers are taken from sources and labeled by origin. Result: a common list of questions, contradictions and gaps, from which the plan of the spec is built. The idea is taken from STORM: the system first finds different viewpoints, then asks questions from each and relies on sources. The roles themselves were chosen here for the project.

- Steps: 4. Desk research
- Source of principles: https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14207v1

## e13 · Limits of the check

From an analysis of the evaluation of early ideas (a student project, a single case): the final choice often remains “by gut feeling”, even with tools. Participants' silence may mean misunderstanding, not low value of the idea. A quantitative check requires more people than most teams have.

- Steps: 7. Measurement; 8. Decision
- Source of principles: none, not checked against a primary source
