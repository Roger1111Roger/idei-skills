# The roast: topic gate and questions

The roast comes before any method is chosen. Its job is to get a concrete topic and enough detail about it. Without them the ideas turn out generic.

## Gate: the topic

A topic is: what the idea is about (an area, a problem or a goal). If the user gave none, say plainly that without a topic there is nothing to work on and ask for it. Help with prompts such as "what annoyed you this week?", "what do you do by hand again and again?", "what would you like to be different?". Do not go on until a topic is given. Do not invent a topic and do not take one from guesses.

## Questions about the topic

Ask 5 to 10 questions in total, in two small batches, tailored to the topic. Skip what the user has already said; if they gave a lot, confirm and sharpen the details instead of asking again.

Batch A (up to four, one message): who the idea is for (a person, a role, a group); the concrete situation or pain, as one real episode; what exists today and what was already tried; what would count as success.

Batch B (up to four, one message, built from the answers): follow up on every vague or contradictory answer; then, if still unknown: constraints (time, money, team size); assets (skills, tools, audience, data); what is off limits; examples the user likes or dislikes; how bold the ideas may be (safe, bold, as wild as possible).

Always ask in batch B, one line: the mode, **light** (one method), **middle** (three methods, the default) or **deep** (a method from each of the 14 approaches), and say that each next mode costs several times more tokens than the previous one.

## Unknown answers

- If the user writes "don't know" or skips a detail question, fill it in yourself with a plausible, deliberately specific assumption. Free imagination is allowed here. Mark every such line `[assumed]`.
- Never do this for the topic itself.
- Assumptions about success and about what is off limits must be conservative (no hidden bans, no invented targets).
- Show the final context block once (user answers and assumed lines) and go on without waiting. The user may correct any line; if so, rerun only the affected part.
